Albert Einstein developed the theory of relativity, one of the two pillars of modern physics. His mass-energy equivalence formula E = mc^2 is the world's most famous equation.
Marie Curie was a Polish and naturalized-French physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize.
Isaac Newton was an English mathematician, physicist, astronomer, alchemist, and author widely recognised as one of the greatest mathematicians and most influential scientists of all time.
The speed of light in a vacuum is exactly 299,792,458 meters per second. This is approximately 300,000 kilometers per second or 186,000 miles per second.
The chemical symbol for gold is Au. Its atomic number is 79, making it one of the higher atomic number elements that occur naturally.
